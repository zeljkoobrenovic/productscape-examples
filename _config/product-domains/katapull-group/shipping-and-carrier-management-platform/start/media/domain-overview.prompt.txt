Draw the picture described below for the top of a product-domain website.
Domain: Shipping and Carrier Management Platform
Reader: a product manager or engineer new to this domain who needs the big picture in ten seconds.

Background (for understanding only; do not write it in the image):
What the domain is: DeliveryMatch, Katapull's order and shipping platform: dynamic checkout, multi-carrier booking across 200+ carriers, transport management, tracking, returns and freight audit for webshops, wholesalers, 3PLs and brokers.
Customers: Merchants and Shippers: E-commerce Operations Manager, Logistics Manager at a Manufacturer or Wholesaler; Logistics Service Providers: Fulfilment and 3PL Operations Lead, Carrier or Transport Broker Partner; Software and Ecosystem Partners: Software Integration Partner; Recipients: Online Shopper and Parcel Recipient
Products: DeliveryMatch Shipping Platform (B2B SaaS - multi-carrier shipping and transport management): The core order and shipping platform: one cloud control layer between order systems, 200+ carriers and logistics partners for B2C and B2B, parcels and pallets,…; DeliveryMatch Dynamic Checkout (B2B SaaS add-on - checkout delivery options): Real-time, stock-aware delivery and pickup options with price, date and CO2 in the webshop checkout; presented as an advanced module; DeliveryMatch Branded Track & Trace (B2B SaaS feature - post-purchase communication): Branded e-mail, SMS and WhatsApp notifications and tracking pages per sales channel, plus exception alerts and carrier performance insight; DeliveryMatch Returns Management (B2B SaaS feature - returns): Returns portal, reason codes, return methods and cost rules, labels and tracking; complemented by Returnista inside the Katapull group; DeliveryMatch Carrier Invoice Control (B2B SaaS add-on - freight audit): Automatic comparison of booked and invoiced transport cost with discrepancy handling (also called the Freight Audit module); DeliveryMatch for Carriers and Brokers (B2B SaaS - transport management for carriers and brokers): Use of the platform by carriers and transport brokers: connect customers' systems, calculate rates and capacity per customer, manage subcontracted carriers and…; DeliveryMatch API and Integration Partner Programme (Platform API / partner programme): REST API, webhooks and partnership for software and system providers - including Katapull siblings - to embed 200+ carriers, rules, labels, tracking, returns a…; DeliveryMatch Shopify App (App marketplace listing): Shopify app (listed since August 2019, shown as free to install) that routes Shopify orders to carriers with shipping rules, checkout options, pickup points an…
Product bricks: 24 building blocks in groups: Shipping Decisions and Execution, Order Intake and System Integration, Delivery Promise and Checkout, Post-Purchase Experience
Data: Orders, Recipients and Shipments, Commercial and Carrier Data, Configuration and Access
Delivery channels: Web Applications: DeliveryMatch Dashboard, Recipient Tracking and Returns Pages; Embedded Components and Plugins: Checkout Widget and Webshop Plugins, Shopify App Store; APIs and Integration Services: Public API and Webhooks, Connector and Event Services; On-site Agents: Print Client
External systems: Sales channels: Webshop platforms, Marketplaces; Hardware devices: Label printers and barcode; Partner: Software providers' WMS, ERP, Partner systems receiving events, Fulfilment partners, suppliers, Parcel carriers; System: ERP systems, WMS systems, Katapull sibling products; Data: ERP, WMS and store systems, Carrier location services, National postcode and address; Service: E-mail, SMS and WhatsApp

Composition:
One wide "context map" read from left to right:
- Left, outside the boundary: each customer group as ONE small cluster of two or three generic people
  with exactly one label, the group name. Do not split a group into individual roles or label single people.
- Center: one large rounded boundary for the domain. Inside it, the products as a row of distinct
  product cards on top; below them ONE shared row of building-block piles, one pile per product brick
  group, spanning the full width under all products. Products are assembled from many groups and groups
  are shared by many products, so do not pair a product with a group: no columns and no product-to-group
  arrows; the product row simply rests on the brick row. Below the bricks a shared data layer (a simple
  database shape) used by all brick groups.
- Along the bottom edge of the boundary: the delivery channels as a base strip of runtime platforms
  (servers, cloud, app stores) that the bricks are deployed to and the products are delivered through.
- Right, outside the boundary: the external systems as separate service tiles, each connected to the
  boundary with a two-way connector line.
- Bottom right, set apart and smaller: competitors as alternative storefronts the customers could choose instead,
  with a thin dashed line towards the customers, not connected to the domain.
- Arrows: customers <-> products; the brick row reads and writes the data layer;
  bricks are deployed onto the delivery channels;
  the domain exchanges data with the external systems. No other arrows.
