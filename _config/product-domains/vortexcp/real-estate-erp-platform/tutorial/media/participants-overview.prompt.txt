Use case: scientific-educational
Asset type: one illustration in a plain-language product-domain tutorial for adults.
Domain: Keeping property, people, money, and maintenance connected
Reader: A curious adult from another field. No knowledge of property management, accounting, tenancy law, or business software is assumed.
Scope: An introduction to the ongoing management of property and the software supporting it. The recurring example is a Dutch apartment building with rental homes and an owners' association. Commercial leases and other countries, including Germany, have different rules. Buying an investment, mortgage advice, and detailed legal procedures are outside this guide. Canal Court, its people, contracts, prices, and financial examples are fictional.
Subject: The people involved and how they relate
The tenant or occupier: Uses the premises under an agreement and communicates about payments, access, and problems. Sam rents apartment 2 at fictional Canal Court. Cares about: A usable home, understandable charges, fair treatment, privacy, and clear responses when something goes wrong.
The owner or investor: Owns the property interest and makes decisions within the applicable rules. Ari owns and lets apartment 2. Cares about: Reliable income, the condition of the property, costs, and responsibilities. An asset manager may help an investor make decisions across several properties.
The property manager: Handles agreed administration and coordination for an owner. Mira manages Ari's rental; her authority comes from the management arrangement. Cares about: Clear responsibilities, accurate records, prompt service, and fees that cover the work. She needs to know which decisions she can make and which require approval.
The owners' association: Coordinates shared interests in an apartment building. In the Netherlands it is called a vereniging van eigenaars, or VvE. Apartment owners are its members. Cares about: Shared maintenance, contributions, reserves, and properly recorded decisions. It is distinct from an individual landlord and from a firm hired to administer it.
Contractors and other suppliers: Inspect, repair, clean, or maintain property under an agreed scope. They need access and someone authorized to instruct the work. Cares about: A clear problem description, safe access, timely decisions, and payment for work that has been properly agreed and checked.
The software provider: Supplies the system and services such as setup, migration, training, and support. Owners or management organizations pay for its use. Cares about: Dependable records and workflows that customers can use effectively. Property rent and the provider's software fees are different revenue streams.

Teaching scene:
An extremely clear relationship map. One large card on the LEFT labeled 'Property manager' shows a woman at a desk. On the RIGHT, five small cards in a vertical column from top to bottom, labeled 'Tenant', 'Owner', 'Owners association', 'Contractor', 'Software provider'. Their simple icons respectively show a home user, person with property document, owners around a roof drawing, repair worker, laptop. Exactly FIVE separate plain lines fan from the manager card's right edge to the five right-hand cards, ONE line per card. No arrowheads, no second line to any card, no connecting the right-hand cards to each other. Above each line one label, respectively: 'Requests and updates', 'Mandate and reports', 'Shared-building coordination', 'Work coordination', 'Tools and support'. Keep lines and labels from crossing. No other words, numbers, charts, money, approval marks or decorative connectors. Large type and generous white space, navy teal amber on warm white. This is a coordination map, not an ownership or spending-authority chart.

Style and composition:
- Aspect ratio 16:9, generous margins, bright warm-white background.
- A polished adult educational illustration: flat editorial drawing, crisp navy outlines,
  restrained teal, blue, and warm amber accents, subtle texture, very little shading.
- Make the concept understandable at the width of a reading column and on a phone.
- Use a small number of recognizable objects and people. Keep relationships spatially clear.
- Use an inclusive cast without occupational or cultural stereotypes.
- Short everyday labels are welcome where they teach the distinction. Use only labels
  explicitly requested in the teaching scene; otherwise keep the image free of text.
- Arrows must have a clear meaning and direction; do not connect unrelated comparison
  panels as though they are required stages. Do not imply certainty, causality, proportions,
  cash movements, or guaranteed outcomes that the explanation does not support.
- Keep all important content inside the frame. No headline, watermark, brand logo,
  decorative charts, invented statistics, jargon, tiny writing, photorealism, or 3D rendering.
- Explain the idea visually. The surrounding tutorial provides its full definition and caveats.
