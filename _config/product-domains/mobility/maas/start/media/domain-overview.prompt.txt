Draw the picture described below for the top of a product-domain website.
Domain: Mobility-as-a-Service (MaaS)
Reader: a product manager or engineer new to this domain who needs the big picture in ten seconds.

Background (for understanding only; do not write it in the image):
What the domain is: The products, workflows, and platform capabilities required to run parking, curbside, public transport payments, fleet mobility, city-planning intelligence, and partner ecosystems at global urban-mobility scale.
Customers: Parking Operators and Asset Owners: Municipal Parking Director; City Authorities and Urban Mobility Planners: Urban Mobility Policy Director; Public Transport Authorities and Operators: Transit Revenue and Operations Executive; Fleets and Businesses: Fleet Mobility and Expense Manager; Mobility Ecosystem Partners: Mobility Platform Partner Manager
Products: Parking and Curb Operations Platform (B2B / B2G Operations); City Mobility Intelligence (B2G Analytics and Planning); Transit Payments and Revenue Operations (B2B / B2G Transit Platform); Fleet and Business Mobility Hub (B2B SaaS); Mobility Partner Exchange (Partner Platform)
Product bricks: 29 building blocks in groups: Platform Foundation, Parking Operations, Partner Ecosystem, Transit Operations
Data: People, Accounts, and Access Data, Commercial and Financial Data, Product, Catalog, and Content Data, Risk, Governance, Security, and Compliance Data, Core Domain Records
Delivery channels: Public Cloud: Parking and Curb Control Account, City Policy and Planning Account, Fare and Device Modernization Account, Fleet and Business Control Account, Partner Onboarding and Monetization Account, Shared Platform Services Account; Mobile App Stores: iOS App Store, Android Play Store
External systems: Back Office: Back-Office Policy Systems, Accounting or ERP Systems, ERP and Operational Systems, Back-Office Systems; Compliance: Compliance and Regulatory Services, Compliance Screening Services; Platform: Event Broker or Messaging Infrastructure, Search Infrastructure; Financial Network: Payment Schemes and PSPs; Identity providers: Corporate Identity Provider; Analytics Platform: Data Warehouse and BI Platforms; Operations: Alerting and Incident Platforms; External Integration: Partner Platforms; Workflow: CRM and Case Management Tools; Messaging services: Notification and Communication Platforms, Notification Delivery Platforms; Data Source: Content and Listing Feeds; Sales channels: Web and Mobile Clients; Traffic Source: Marketing and Acquisition Platforms; Transaction Consumer: Checkout and Billing Flows; Finance Consumer: Finance and Reporting Systems; Identity Consumer: Customer and Operator Applications; Control Consumer: Audit and Risk Systems; Insight Consumer: Operational Teams and Workspaces; Analytics Consumer: Reporting and Planning Systems; Integration Consumer: Internal Domain Services; Partner Consumer: Partner Operations; Decision Consumer: Operational Decision Flows; Policy Consumer: Administration and Governance Tools; Workflow Consumer: Operator Workflows; Journey Consumer: Customer and Partner Touchpoints; Service Consumer: Business Workflows; Operations Consumer: Operations and Support Teams; Experience Consumer: Consumer Experience Surfaces; Business Consumer: Merchandising and Growth Tools
Competitors: 5 players, for example Uber, Moovit, Masabi

Composition:
One wide "context map" read from left to right:
- Left, outside the boundary: each customer group as ONE small cluster of two or three generic people
  with exactly one label, the group name. Do not split a group into individual roles or label single people.
- Center: one large rounded boundary for the domain. Inside it, the products as a row of distinct
  product cards on top; below them ONE shared row of building-block piles, one pile per product brick
  group, spanning the full width under all products. Products are assembled from many groups and groups
  are shared by many products, so do not pair a product with a group: no columns and no product-to-group
  arrows; the product row simply rests on the brick row. Below the bricks a shared data layer (a simple
  database shape) used by all brick groups.
- Along the bottom edge of the boundary: the delivery channels as a base strip of runtime platforms
  (servers, cloud, app stores) that the bricks are deployed to and the products are delivered through.
- Right, outside the boundary: the external systems as separate service tiles, each connected to the
  boundary with a two-way connector line.
- Bottom right, set apart and smaller: competitors as alternative storefronts the customers could choose instead,
  with a thin dashed line towards the customers, not connected to the domain.
- Arrows: customers <-> products; the brick row reads and writes the data layer; bricks are deployed onto the delivery channels; the domain exchanges data with the external systems. No other arrows.

Style:
- Aspect ratio 16:9, generous margins, everything inside the frame.
- Background: pure white (#FFFFFF) across the whole canvas, with no cream, beige, paper tint,
  texture, or vignette. Only the domain boundary and the tiles may have a light fill.
- Clean flat editorial illustration with isometric hints: crisp navy outlines, restrained teal, blue,
  and warm amber accents, soft shadows, no photorealism and no 3D rendering.
- Outside elements and inside elements must be clearly distinguishable: the boundary separates them.
- Labels in a clear sans-serif, large enough to read at half size on a laptop screen.
- Generic, inclusive people and generic devices. No brand logos, trademarks, or watermarks anywhere,
  even for the named competitors, sales channels, and external systems: draw generic icons
  (a shopping cart, a storefront, a truck) instead of company marks. This includes app-store, browser,
  payment-card, and social-network marks, and product cards drawn as wordmarks.
