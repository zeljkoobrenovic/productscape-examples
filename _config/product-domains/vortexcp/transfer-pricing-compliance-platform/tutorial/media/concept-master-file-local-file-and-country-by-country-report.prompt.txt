Use case: scientific-educational
Asset type: one illustration in a plain-language product-domain tutorial for adults.
Domain: Transfer pricing: how companies explain prices inside a group
Reader: A curious adult arriving from another field. No background in tax, accounting, law, or business software is assumed.
Scope: An introduction to cross-border transfer pricing and the people, records, and software behind its annual documentation cycle. It uses the common framework of the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD), with an EU example in the history. Countries set their own legal requirements; OECD guidance is not a worldwide filing law. Detailed tax calculations, customs duties, and country-specific filing instructions are outside this guide. Luma, its people, countries A and B, and all amounts are fictional teaching examples.
Subject: Master file, local file, and country-by-country report
Meaning: The OECD documentation framework contains three views. A master file explains the group as a whole. A local file examines relevant transactions of a local company, including the analysis and financial information. A country-by-country report summarizes information such as revenue, profit, tax, and activity by country for groups within its scope. It is often shortened to CbCR.
Concrete example: Think of a group portrait, a close-up of Luma's seller, and a country summary table. They answer different questions. Local law determines which documents are required, their contents, and whether they are filed routinely or kept ready for a request.
Why this matters: CbCR helps authorities identify risks; it does not prove an individual price is right. Public country-by-country reporting, such as the EU regime, is a separate disclosure obligation with its own rules.

Teaching scene:
Three equally sized side-by-side document covers, not nested and not linked in a mandatory sequence. Cover 1 title 'Master file', subtitle 'Group overview', simple icon of several linked companies. Cover 2 title 'Local file', subtitle 'Local transactions', magnifying glass on one company and invoice. Cover 3 title 'Country-by-country report', subtitle 'Country summaries', simple abstract table with no real figures. Small shared footer 'Requirements depend on local rules'. Exactly these seven labels and no other text. Do not show every company filing all three, no public website globe, no confidential lock that implies all documents always confidential, no checkmarks.

Style and composition:
- Aspect ratio 16:9, generous margins, bright warm-white background.
- A polished adult educational illustration: flat editorial drawing, crisp navy outlines,
  restrained teal, blue, and warm amber accents, subtle texture, very little shading.
- Make the concept understandable at the width of a reading column and on a phone.
- Use a small number of recognizable objects and people. Keep relationships spatially clear.
- Use an inclusive cast without occupational or cultural stereotypes.
- Short everyday labels are welcome where they teach the distinction. Use only labels
  explicitly requested in the teaching scene; otherwise keep the image free of text.
- Arrows must have a clear meaning and direction; do not connect unrelated comparison
  panels as though they are required stages. Do not imply certainty, causality, proportions,
  cash movements, or guaranteed outcomes that the explanation does not support.
- Keep all important content inside the frame. No headline, watermark, brand logo,
  decorative charts, invented statistics, jargon, tiny writing, photorealism, or 3D rendering.
- Explain the idea visually. The surrounding tutorial provides its full definition and caveats.
