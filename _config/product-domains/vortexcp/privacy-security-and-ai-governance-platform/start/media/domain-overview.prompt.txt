Draw the picture described below for the top of a product-domain website.
Domain: Privacy, Security and AI Governance Platform
Reader: a product manager or engineer new to this domain who needs the big picture in ten seconds.

Background (for understanding only; do not write it in the image):
What the domain is: Models the subscription software and expert support used to keep processing records current, assess privacy and AI risks, handle rights requests and breaches, govern suppliers, and demonstrate information security controls. Based on PrivacyPerfect, the domain connects accountable specialists, business contributors, buying sponsors, advisers, administrators and affected individuals through reusable evidence, review workflows and a governed SaaS service. Architecture, staffing and strategic targets are explicit modeling assumptions.
Customers: Accountability and Assurance: Privacy Officer, Information Security Manager, AI Governance Owner, Executive Sponsor; Distributed Contribution and Administration: Business Process Owner, Supplier Risk Manager, Tenant Administrator; Expert Delivery Partners: External Privacy Adviser; People Exercising Rights: Individual Rights Requester
Products: Privacy Management (B2B / B2G SaaS — observed solution area); Information Security Management (B2B / B2G SaaS — observed solution area); Third Party Risk Management (B2B SaaS — observed solution area); AI Risk Management (B2B / B2G SaaS — observed solution area); Expert-Assisted Governance Delivery (Modeled service bundle — dedicated adviser packaging unverified)
Product bricks: 30 building blocks in groups: Shared Trust and Delivery, Privacy Operations, Security, Supplier and AI Assurance
Data: Processing and Privacy, Assurance and Relationships, Shared Trust and Operations
Delivery channels: Governed SaaS Runtime: Privacy Operations Runtime, Assurance Runtime, Shared Trust Runtime, Service and Expert Delivery Runtime; Browser Access: Authenticated Organization Workspace, Organization Public Register and Rights Intake, Client-Scoped Adviser Workspace
External systems: Integration: Customer operational data systems, Supervisory authority submission channels, Customer identity provider, Approved notification provider
Competitors: 9 players, for example OneTrust, TrustArc, DataGuard

Composition:
One wide "context map" read from left to right:
- Left, outside the boundary: each customer group as ONE small cluster of two or three generic people
  with exactly one label, the group name. Do not split a group into individual roles or label single people.
- Center: one large rounded boundary for the domain. Inside it, the products as a row of distinct
  product cards on top; below them ONE shared row of building-block piles, one pile per product brick
  group, spanning the full width under all products. Products are assembled from many groups and groups
  are shared by many products, so do not pair a product with a group: no columns and no product-to-group
  arrows; the product row simply rests on the brick row. Below the bricks a shared data layer (a simple
  database shape) used by all brick groups.
- Along the bottom edge of the boundary: the delivery channels as a base strip of runtime platforms
  (servers, cloud, app stores) that the bricks are deployed to and the products are delivered through.
- Right, outside the boundary: the external systems as separate service tiles, each connected to the
  boundary with a two-way connector line.
- Bottom right, set apart and smaller: competitors as alternative storefronts the customers could choose instead,
  with a thin dashed line towards the customers, not connected to the domain.
- Arrows: customers <-> products; the brick row reads and writes the data layer; bricks are deployed onto the delivery channels; the domain exchanges data with the external systems. No other arrows.

- Background: pure white (#FFFFFF) across the whole canvas, with no cream, beige, paper tint,

  and warm amber accents, soft shadows, no photorealism and no 3D rendering.
- Outside elements and inside elements must be clearly distinguishable: the boundary separates them.
- Labels in a clear sans-serif, large enough to read at half size on a laptop screen.
