Use case: scientific-educational
Asset type: one illustration in a plain-language product-domain tutorial for adults.
Domain: Keeping property, people, money, and maintenance connected
Reader: A curious adult from another field. No knowledge of property management, accounting, tenancy law, or business software is assumed.
Scope: An introduction to the ongoing management of property and the software supporting it. The recurring example is a Dutch apartment building with rental homes and an owners' association. Commercial leases and other countries, including Germany, have different rules. Buying an investment, mortgage advice, and detailed legal procedures are outside this guide. Canal Court, its people, contracts, prices, and financial examples are fictional.
Subject: Mira manages Sam's tenancy and a roof leak
Setup: Canal Court has four separately owned apartments and shared building parts. Ari lets apartment 2 to Sam; Mira manages that rental. Assume the building documents assign the roof work in this example to the owners' association. The association has its own decision process and accounts. All amounts and arrangements below are illustrative.
Step 1: Identify the place, parties, and authority. Mira links apartment 2 to the building, Ari's ownership, and her management arrangement. She records the relevant association contacts and who can authorize different kinds of work. Why: Owning, living in, and managing the same place are different roles. Being able to edit a record does not itself authorize spending. Possible complication: None specified.
Step 2: Record the tenancy and hand over the home. After the relevant checks, the parties agree the tenancy. Mira records its dates, the illustrative €900 rent and €100 service advance, and the deposit separately. The handover records the property's condition, keys, and contact arrangements. Why: The recurring administration should reflect the actual agreement, with useful evidence of the starting condition. Possible complication: None specified.
Step 3: Charge correctly and match the money. The system produces the €1,000 monthly charge. A €950 bank receipt leaves €50 to investigate. In this case Sam identifies a transfer mistake and sends the remaining €50; Mira matches both receipts to the correct charge. Why: The resolution comes from checking the records and communicating, rather than treating a mismatch as automatic proof of misconduct. Possible complication: None specified.
Step 4: Investigate the reported leak. Sam reports ceiling water. Mira assesses urgency through the agreed process, coordinates an inspection, and keeps Sam informed. The contractor identifies a shared-roof problem, so she involves the association's authorized representative. Why: The report belongs with Sam's home, while the repair also belongs with a shared building part and its decision-makers. Possible complication: An urgent problem needs the applicable emergency response, while responsibility and cost allocation are recorded. The system should preserve both the resident's report and the linked shared-building work.
Step 5: Authorize, carry out, and verify the work. The association arranges the shared work under its procedures. Mira coordinates access and updates Sam. After repair, the agreed result and any remaining damage are checked. The appropriate reviewer checks the supplier bill against the approved work. Why: An instruction, a contractor's completion message, an accepted result, and a paid bill are separate milestones. Possible complication: None specified.
Step 6: Close the records and plan ahead. Mira records the outcome for Sam and Ari, while the association updates its own costs and maintenance plan. In the later annual service-cost review, €1,200 of Sam's advances is compared with €1,080 of permitted, properly allocated costs, producing a €120 credit to settle. Why: Ari's rental finances, the association's roof spending, and Sam's service-cost statement remain distinguishable. The roof expense is not automatically passed on to Sam. Possible complication: None specified.
Outcome: Sam has a repaired home and understandable records. Ari can see the rental's financial position, and the association can account for shared work and future needs. Mira can trace the request, decisions, work, and bills without confusing one party's money or responsibilities with another's.
