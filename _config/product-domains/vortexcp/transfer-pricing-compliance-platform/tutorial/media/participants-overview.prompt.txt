Use case: scientific-educational
Asset type: one illustration in a plain-language product-domain tutorial for adults.
Domain: Transfer pricing: how companies explain prices inside a group
Reader: A curious adult arriving from another field. No background in tax, accounting, law, or business software is assumed.
Scope: An introduction to cross-border transfer pricing and the people, records, and software behind its annual documentation cycle. It uses the common framework of the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD), with an EU example in the history. Countries set their own legal requirements; OECD guidance is not a worldwide filing law. Detailed tax calculations, customs duties, and country-specific filing instructions are outside this guide. Luma, its people, countries A and B, and all amounts are fictional teaching examples.
Subject: The people involved and how they relate
Business and local finance teams: Run the factory or selling company and record sales, expenses, and internal charges. They know what actually happened. Cares about: Accurate accounts, manageable requests, and descriptions that reflect their work. In our example, Leo manages the selling company's finances.
The group's tax team: Coordinates the explanation across companies, obtains expert review, and plans the reporting work. Sofia leads this work at fictional Luma. Cares about: Tax treatment the group can explain, deadlines, and consistency across countries. The group funds the software and any outside help.
Legal colleagues and company decision-makers: Maintain company and contract records and approve decisions within their responsibilities. Cares about: Agreements that reflect real arrangements and a clear record of who authorized changes.
Tax advisers and platform providers: Supply specialist analysis, software, or document preparation. One provider may offer both software and services. Cares about: Clear scope, reliable inputs, review time, and fees that cover the work. Outsourcing preparation still leaves the company with responsibilities.
Tax authorities: Administer local tax rules, assess risk, and examine selected cases. Each country's authority has its own role. Cares about: Correct taxable profit and useful explanations. Receiving a report does not mean accepting every position in it.

Teaching scene:
Create a simple adult learning relationship map, not an organization chart or a money-flow diagram. Center: a woman coordinating documents, labeled 'Tax team'. Four surrounding separate illustrated cards: 'Business and finance' with a lamp factory and ledger; 'Legal team' with a contract; 'Advisers and platform' with a person and laptop; 'Tax authorities' with two separate office buildings. Four clear single-headed arrows only: Business and finance TO Tax team labeled 'Facts'; Legal team TO Tax team labeled 'Agreements'; Advisers and platform TO Tax team labeled 'Analysis and tools'; Tax team TO Tax authorities labeled 'Required reports'. No arrows between any other nodes, no government approval ticks, no currency, no flags, no corporate ownership lines. White space, navy and teal with restrained amber. Exactly these nine short labels, no other text. The relationships show contributions to preparation, not a complete legal filing map.

Style and composition:
- Aspect ratio 16:9, generous margins, bright warm-white background.
- A polished adult educational illustration: flat editorial drawing, crisp navy outlines,
  restrained teal, blue, and warm amber accents, subtle texture, very little shading.
- Make the concept understandable at the width of a reading column and on a phone.
- Use a small number of recognizable objects and people. Keep relationships spatially clear.
- Use an inclusive cast without occupational or cultural stereotypes.
- Short everyday labels are welcome where they teach the distinction. Use only labels
  explicitly requested in the teaching scene; otherwise keep the image free of text.
- Arrows must have a clear meaning and direction; do not connect unrelated comparison
  panels as though they are required stages. Do not imply certainty, causality, proportions,
  cash movements, or guaranteed outcomes that the explanation does not support.
- Keep all important content inside the frame. No headline, watermark, brand logo,
  decorative charts, invented statistics, jargon, tiny writing, photorealism, or 3D rendering.
- Explain the idea visually. The surrounding tutorial provides its full definition and caveats.
