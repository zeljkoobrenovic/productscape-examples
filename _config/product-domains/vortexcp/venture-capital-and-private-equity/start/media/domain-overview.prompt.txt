Draw the picture described below for the top of a product-domain website.
Domain: Venture Capital and Private Equity
Reader: a product manager or engineer new to this domain who needs the big picture in ten seconds.

Background (for understanding only; do not write it in the image):
What the domain is: Vortex-informed software investment products connecting fund investors, founders, and portfolio management through capital, active ownership, and realized value.
Customers: Company owners: Software Founder Planning an Ownership Transition, High-growth Software Founder; Portfolio management: Portfolio Company Management Team; Capital providers: Professional Private-markets Fund Allocator; Investment operators: Investment Professional and Committee Contributor
Products: Buyout and Ownership Partnership (Capital and active-ownership service); Venture and Growth Partnership (Conditional mandate: capital and growth support); Portfolio Value Creation Partnership (Management support and expert services); Fund Participation and Investor Service (Vehicle-specific investment and reporting relationship); Investment and Stewardship Workbench (Internal operating product)
Product bricks: 23 building blocks in groups: Investment Lifecycle, Portfolio Value Creation, Fund and Investor Services, Technology and Stewardship
Data: Investment Lifecycle, Portfolio Value Creation, Technology and Stewardship, Fund and Investor Services
Delivery channels: Permissioned Workflow Delivery: Investment Case Workspace, Company Support and Stewardship Workspace, Fund Finance and Investor Service Workspace; Assisted Partnership Delivery: Founder and Transaction Advisory, Company Workshops and Board Reviews, Authorized Investor Report Exchange
External systems: Evidence Exchange: Deal CRM and authorized referral, Virtual data rooms and advisor, Authorized legal, financial, and, Company capitalization records
Competitors: 8 players, for example Hg, Constellation Software, Main Capital Partners

Composition:
One wide "context map" read from left to right:
- Left, outside the boundary: each customer group as ONE small cluster of two or three generic people
  with exactly one label, the group name. Do not split a group into individual roles or label single people.
- Center: one large rounded boundary for the domain. Inside it, the products as a row of distinct
  product cards on top; below them ONE shared row of building-block piles, one pile per product brick
  group, spanning the full width under all products. Products are assembled from many groups and groups
  are shared by many products, so do not pair a product with a group: no columns and no product-to-group
  arrows; the product row simply rests on the brick row. Below the bricks a shared data layer (a simple
  database shape) used by all brick groups.
- Along the bottom edge of the boundary: the delivery channels as a base strip of runtime platforms
  (servers, cloud, app stores) that the bricks are deployed to and the products are delivered through.
- Right, outside the boundary: the external systems as separate service tiles, each connected to the
  boundary with a two-way connector line.
- Bottom right, set apart and smaller: competitors as alternative storefronts the customers could choose instead,
  with a thin dashed line towards the customers, not connected to the domain.
- Arrows: customers <-> products; the brick row reads and writes the data layer;
  bricks are deployed onto the delivery channels;
  the domain exchanges data with the external systems. No other arrows.
