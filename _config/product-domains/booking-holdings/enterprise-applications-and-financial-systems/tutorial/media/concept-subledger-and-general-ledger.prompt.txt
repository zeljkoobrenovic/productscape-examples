Use case: scientific-educational
Asset type: one illustration in a plain-language product-domain tutorial for adults.
Domain: Following the money: how a travel company's financial systems work
Reader: A curious adult from a different field who has never worked in accounting, payments or enterprise software. No knowledge of bookkeeping, SAP or software architecture is assumed.
Scope: An introduction to the financial systems behind a large online travel company: how a booking turns into payments, payouts, revenue, taxes and published financial statements, and why the software that does this needs deliberate design. Booking Holdings (Booking.com, Priceline, Agoda, KAYAK, OpenTable) is the running context, and facts about it come from its public filings and job postings. The guesthouse booking, the people and the amounts in the walkthrough are fictional. Traveler-facing apps, marketing and human-resources systems are outside this guide. Accounting and tax rules differ by country; the explanation describes general patterns for a United States-listed company that operates heavily in Europe.
Subject: Subledger and general ledger
Meaning: The general ledger holds totals by account and is the source for financial statements. A subledger holds the detail underneath one area, such as every booking-level payment, refund and payout, and periodically passes summarized totals to the general ledger.
Concrete example: A subledger might hold tens of millions of individual booking payments for a month. The general ledger receives a few thousand summarized entries, each traceable back to the detailed records.
Why this matters: Putting every booking directly into the general ledger would make it slow and unreadable. Keeping detail in a subledger keeps the main books manageable, but only if every summary can be traced back to its details. Booking.com's finance-systems job postings mention SAP's high-volume contract accounting and billing tools (FI-CA and BRIM) for this kind of work.

Teaching scene:
A funnel diagram: at the top, a wide pile of many small receipt cards labeled 'Subledger: every booking'; they flow down through a funnel labeled 'summarize' into a single neat book at the bottom labeled 'General ledger'. A thin dotted line runs from the book back up to one specific receipt card, labeled 'trace back'. Only these labels.
