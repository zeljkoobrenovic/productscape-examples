Draw the picture described below for the top of a product-domain website.
Domain: PE Technology Capability
Reader: a product manager or engineer new to this domain who needs the big picture in ten seconds.

Background (for understanding only; do not write it in the image):
What the domain is: A proposed Technology Value Creation Platform connecting investment decisions, portfolio execution, shared learning, and exit readiness.
Customers: Investment Decisions: Investment Partner, Deal Team, Investment Committee; Portfolio Leadership and Governance: Portfolio CEO, Portfolio CTO / CPTO, Portfolio Board; Exit and Investor Confidence: Exit Team, Limited Partner
Products: Technology Market Lens (Internal investment research service): A concise market and customer-value view for pre-diligence sourcing decisions; Technology Investment Assessment (Internal assessment and specialist service): A seven-dimension assessment of technology against the investment thesis; Technology Investment Thesis (Internal decision artifact and advisory service): A one-page technology case with economic options, uncertainties, committee conditions, and value evidence for revisiting decisions; Technology 100-Day Plan (Company alignment and planning service): Three to five company-owned priorities carrying diligence into measurable ownership outcomes; CTO Partnership (Opt-in operating partnership): A trusted coach and co-pilot relationship for difficult decisions and blocked initiatives; Technology Accelerators (Bounded specialist interventions): Minimum viable diagnostics, benchmarks, decision frameworks, and actions for the ten accelerators in the input; Vortex Portfolio Technology Intelligence (Permissioned portfolio learning service): Contextual company measurements and reusable patterns with cohort and disclosure controls; CTO Community and Expert Network (Opt-in peer and specialist ecosystem): Suitable peers, leadership candidates, and specialist expertise connected to an accepted company outcome; Technology Integration Partnership (Company acquisition and scaling support): Platform, team, and data integration decisions with company-owned benefit and continuity tests; Technology Exit Readiness (Continuous readiness and buyer evidence service): An evidenced technology narrative and approved answers to buyer diligence questions; Technology Operating Capability Evidence (Approved board and allocator evidence service): Qualified evidence of adoption, company outcomes, and repeatable contribution with attribution limits
Product bricks: 26 building blocks in groups: Accelerate, Learn, Invest, Scale
Data: Invest, Align, Accelerate, Learn, Scale, Exit
Delivery channels: Proposed Capability Workspaces: Permissioned Capability Records and Workflows; Decision and Support Surfaces: Investment Workbench, Company Partnership Workspace, Portfolio Learning Workspace, Approved Board, Buyer, and LP Evidence Packs
External systems: Evidence Exchange: Public company, market, and customer research sources, Authorized company evidence room and specialist assessment exports, Fund investment committee records, Company-approved recruitment and candidate communication systems
Competitors: 3 players, for example Insight Onsite, Bain Technology Due Diligence, Crosslake

Composition:
One wide "context map" read from left to right:
- Left, outside the boundary: each customer group as ONE small cluster of two or three generic people
  with exactly one label, the group name. Do not split a group into individual roles or label single people.
- Center: one large rounded boundary for the domain. Inside it, the products as a row of distinct
  product cards on top; below them ONE shared row of building-block piles, one pile per product brick
  group, spanning the full width under all products. Products are assembled from many groups and groups
  are shared by many products, so do not pair a product with a group: no columns and no product-to-group
  arrows; the product row simply rests on the brick row. Below the bricks a shared data layer (a simple
  database shape) used by all brick groups.
- Along the bottom edge of the boundary: the delivery channels as a base strip of runtime platforms
  (servers, cloud, app stores) that the bricks are deployed to and the products are delivered through.
- Right, outside the boundary: the external systems as separate service tiles, each connected to the
  boundary with a two-way connector line.
- Bottom right, set apart and smaller: competitors as alternative storefronts the customers could choose instead,
  with a thin dashed line towards the customers, not connected to the domain.
- Arrows: customers <-> products; the brick row reads and writes the data layer; bricks are deployed onto the delivery channels; the domain exchanges data with the external systems. No other arrows.

Style:
- Aspect ratio 16:9, generous margins, everything inside the frame.
- Background: pure white (#FFFFFF) across the whole canvas, with no cream, beige, paper tint,
  texture, or vignette. Only the domain boundary and the tiles may have a light fill.
- Clean flat editorial illustration with isometric hints: crisp navy outlines, restrained teal, blue,
  and warm amber accents, soft shadows, no photorealism and no 3D rendering.
- Outside elements and inside elements must be clearly distinguishable: the boundary separates them.
- Labels in a clear sans-serif, large enough to read at half size on a laptop screen.
- Generic, inclusive people and generic devices. No brand logos, trademarks, or watermarks anywhere,
  even for the named competitors, sales channels, and external systems: draw generic icons
  (a shopping cart, a storefront, a truck) instead of company marks. This includes app-store, browser,
  payment-card, and social-network marks, and product cards drawn as wordmarks.
