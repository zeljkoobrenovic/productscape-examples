Draw the picture described below for the top of a product-domain website.
Domain: Corporate Learning and Skills Development
Reader: a product manager or engineer new to this domain who needs the big picture in ten seconds.

Background (for understanding only; do not write it in the image):
What the domain is: A corporate learning and skills platform modeled on Studytube: employers connect role requirements, employee development, mandatory qualifications, internal knowledge and external training to measurable workplace outcomes.
Customers: Learning Investment and Governance: Learning and Development Director, Executive HR and Learning Budget Sponsor; Learning Operations and Assurance: Learning Administrator and Qualification Coordinator, IT Integration and Information Security Owner; Learners and Managers: Employee and Flexible Worker Learner, Team Manager and Skills Coach; Content and Training Partners: Subject Matter Expert and Learning Author, External Training Provider Operations Lead
Products: Learning Management System and Employer Academy (B2B SaaS); Skill Navigator and Development Planning (B2B SaaS); AI Authoring and Knowledge Publication (B2B SaaS authoring); Learning Content Libraries and Subscriptions (B2B content subscription); External Training Intermediary (B2B training procurement)
Product bricks: 27 building blocks in groups: Skills and Learner Experience, Training Supply and Value, Trusted Platform and Service, Learning Operations and Knowledge
Data: Identity and Workforce, Skills and Learning Evidence, Learning Operations, Training Supply and Finance, Outcome and Service Evidence
Delivery channels: Managed Service Runtime: Skills and Learner Experience, Learning Operations and Knowledge, Training Supply and Value, Trusted Platform and Service; Browser Applications: Learner and Manager Academy, Learning Administration and Authoring, Training Provider Operations, Implementation, Integration and Support
External systems: Integration: External learning-content provider, Approved communication delivery provider, Employer calendar or virtual classroom, Approved AI model provider; Api Consumer: Authorized employer and training-provider integration clients
Competitors: 8 players, for example Docebo, Cornerstone, SAP SuccessFactors Learning

Composition:
One wide "context map" read from left to right:
- Left, outside the boundary: each customer group as ONE small cluster of two or three generic people
  with exactly one label, the group name. Do not split a group into individual roles or label single people.
- Center: one large rounded boundary for the domain. Inside it, the products as a row of distinct
  product cards on top; below them ONE shared row of building-block piles, one pile per product brick
  group, spanning the full width under all products. Products are assembled from many groups and groups
  are shared by many products, so do not pair a product with a group: no columns and no product-to-group
  arrows; the product row simply rests on the brick row. Below the bricks a shared data layer (a simple
  database shape) used by all brick groups.
- Along the bottom edge of the boundary: the delivery channels as a base strip of runtime platforms
  (servers, cloud, app stores) that the bricks are deployed to and the products are delivered through.
- Right, outside the boundary: the external systems as separate service tiles, each connected to the
  boundary with a two-way connector line.
- Bottom right, set apart and smaller: competitors as alternative storefronts the customers could choose instead,
  with a thin dashed line towards the customers, not connected to the domain.
- Arrows: customers <-> products; the brick row reads and writes the data layer; bricks are deployed onto the delivery channels; the domain exchanges data with the external systems. No other arrows.

Write no other text anywhere: no headline or caption above, below, or beside the boundary, no

write the words overview, diagram, illustration, or picture.

Style:
- Aspect ratio 16:9, generous margins, everything inside the frame.
- Background: pure white (#FFFFFF) across the whole canvas, with no cream, beige, paper tint,
  texture, or vignette. Only the domain boundary and the tiles may have a light fill.
- Clean flat editorial illustration with isometric hints: crisp navy outlines, restrained teal, blue,
  and warm amber accents, soft shadows, no photorealism and no 3D rendering.
- Outside elements and inside elements must be clearly distinguishable: the boundary separates them.
- Labels in a clear sans-serif, large enough to read at half size on a laptop screen.
- Generic, inclusive people and generic devices. No brand logos, trademarks, or watermarks anywhere,
  even for the named competitors, sales channels, and external systems: draw generic icons
  (a shopping cart, a storefront, a truck) instead of company marks. This includes app-store, browser,
  payment-card, and social-network marks, and product cards drawn as wordmarks.
