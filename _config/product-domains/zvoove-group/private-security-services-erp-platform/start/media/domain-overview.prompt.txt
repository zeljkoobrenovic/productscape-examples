Draw the picture described below for the top of a product-domain website.
Domain: Private Security Services ERP Platform
Reader: a product manager or engineer new to this domain who needs the big picture in ten seconds.

Background (for understanding only; do not write it in the image):
What the domain is: zvoove's ERP and workforce platform for private security and event-staffing companies in Spain and Latin America: guard scheduling, licence compliance, time control, payroll and billing.
Customers: Security Company Leadership: Security Company Managing Director, Latin American Security Operator; Operations: Operations and Control Room Coordinator; Frontline Guards: Security Guard; Back Office and Compliance: HR, Payroll and Compliance Officer; Security Clients: Client Security Manager
Products: e-Satellite for Private Security (B2B Vertical ERP SaaS): Freematica's ERP for private-security companies: scheduling, rounds, incidents, multi-convention payroll, presence, invoicing, client portal and Segurpri repor…; e-Satellite Guard App (B2B2E Mobile): Mobile app for vigilantes: roster, instructions, rounds, incidents; Security Client Portal (B2B Portal): Secure client access to documentation, service validations, rounds and incidents; e-Satellite Latin America (Mexico, Colombia) (B2B Vertical ERP SaaS): e-Satellite with country packs for Mexican and Colombian labour, tax and security regulation
Product bricks: 18 building blocks in groups: Security Operations, Workforce and Compliance, Pay, Bill and Insight, Contracts and Commercial
Data: Commercial Data, Operations Data, Workforce and Compliance Data, Finance and Platform Data
Delivery channels: Web Applications: e-Satellite Back Office, Control Room, Client Portal; Mobile: Guard App; Integrations: Open API
External systems: Regulator: Security authorities, Tax authority e-invoicing systems; Distribution: Apple App Store and Google Play; Hardware devices: Clocking terminals; Service: e-Signature providers; Integration Consumer: Customer HR, access-control and accounting systems
Competitors: 7 players, for example Trackforce, Beta10, Galdón Software

Composition:
One wide "context map" read from left to right:
- Left, outside the boundary: each customer group as ONE small cluster of two or three generic people
  with exactly one label, the group name. Do not split a group into individual roles or label single people.
- Center: one large rounded boundary for the domain. Inside it, the products as a row of distinct
  product cards on top; below them ONE shared row of building-block piles, one pile per product brick
  group, spanning the full width under all products. Products are assembled from many groups and groups
  are shared by many products, so do not pair a product with a group: no columns and no product-to-group
  arrows; the product row simply rests on the brick row. Below the bricks a shared data layer (a simple
  database shape) used by all brick groups.
- Along the bottom edge of the boundary: the delivery channels as a base strip of runtime platforms
  (servers, cloud, app stores) that the bricks are deployed to and the products are delivered through.
- Right, outside the boundary: the external systems as separate service tiles, each connected to the
  boundary with a two-way connector line.
- Bottom right, set apart and smaller: competitors as alternative storefronts the customers could choose instead,
  with a thin dashed line towards the customers, not connected to the domain.
- Arrows: customers <-> products; the brick row reads and writes the data layer; bricks are deployed onto the delivery channels; the domain exchanges data with the external systems. No other arrows.

Style:
- Aspect ratio 16:9, generous margins, everything inside the frame.
- Background: pure white (#FFFFFF) across the whole canvas, with no cream, beige, paper tint,
  texture, or vignette. Only the domain boundary and the tiles may have a light fill.
- Clean flat editorial illustration with isometric hints: crisp navy outlines, restrained teal, blue,
  and warm amber accents, soft shadows, no photorealism and no 3D rendering.
- Outside elements and inside elements must be clearly distinguishable: the boundary separates them.
- Labels in a clear sans-serif, large enough to read at half size on a laptop screen.
- Generic, inclusive people and generic devices. No brand logos, trademarks, or watermarks anywhere,
