Use case: scientific-educational
Asset type: one illustration in a plain-language product-domain tutorial for adults.
Domain: Keeping property, people, money, and maintenance connected
Reader: A curious adult from another field. No knowledge of property management, accounting, tenancy law, or business software is assumed.
Scope: An introduction to the ongoing management of property and the software supporting it. The recurring example is a Dutch apartment building with rental homes and an owners' association. Commercial leases and other countries, including Germany, have different rules. Buying an investment, mortgage advice, and detailed legal procedures are outside this guide. Canal Court, its people, contracts, prices, and financial examples are fictional.
Subject: Portfolio, building, and unit
Meaning: A portfolio is a collection of properties managed or considered together. A building is a physical structure. A unit is a separately identified space, such as an apartment, shop, or parking place. Shared parts, such as a roof or stairwell, may need their own records and responsibilities.
Concrete example: Canal Court contains four apartments. Sam lives in unit 2, but the roof serves the building. Ari's ownership, Sam's tenancy, and Mira's management role must be linked to the right place and period.
Why this matters: An address alone may be too broad to identify a contract or cost. The place where a problem appears is not always the part that needs repairing.

Teaching scene:
A simple hierarchy of places, not an ownership diagram. Top small collection of three buildings labeled 'Portfolio'. A line highlights one building below labeled 'Building'. Show that building as a clean cutaway with FOUR apartments, only one highlighted and labeled 'Unit 2'. The shared roof is a single continuous roof across the building, labeled 'Shared roof'. Exactly these four text labels, no other unit numbers, money, owners, contracts or arrows implying ownership. Adult editorial navy teal amber, generous spacing.

Style and composition:
- Aspect ratio 16:9, generous margins, bright warm-white background.
- A polished adult educational illustration: flat editorial drawing, crisp navy outlines,
  restrained teal, blue, and warm amber accents, subtle texture, very little shading.
- Make the concept understandable at the width of a reading column and on a phone.
- Use a small number of recognizable objects and people. Keep relationships spatially clear.
- Use an inclusive cast without occupational or cultural stereotypes.
- Short everyday labels are welcome where they teach the distinction. Use only labels
  explicitly requested in the teaching scene; otherwise keep the image free of text.
- Arrows must have a clear meaning and direction; do not connect unrelated comparison
  panels as though they are required stages. Do not imply certainty, causality, proportions,
  cash movements, or guaranteed outcomes that the explanation does not support.
- Keep all important content inside the frame. No headline, watermark, brand logo,
  decorative charts, invented statistics, jargon, tiny writing, photorealism, or 3D rendering.
- Explain the idea visually. The surrounding tutorial provides its full definition and caveats.
