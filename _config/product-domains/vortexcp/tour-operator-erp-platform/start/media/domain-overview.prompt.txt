Draw the picture described below for the top of a product-domain website.
Domain: Tour Operator ERP Platform
Reader: a product manager or engineer new to this domain who needs the big picture in ten seconds.

Background (for understanding only; do not write it in the image):
What the domain is: Covers the ERP-style software platform that tour operators, group and coach travel providers, tailor-made travel specialists, destination management companies, and travel agencies run their business on: travel product and contract management, visual tour building and quoting, bookings and departures, group operations such as seat allocation, room lists and guide assignment, supplier and reseller portals, real-time bookable customer websites, traveler portals and apps, payments, invoicing, accounting and guarantee-fund compliance, marketing, quality, and business intelligence across the Benelux and wider European travel trade.
Customers: Group and Coach Tour Operators: Group Travel Operations Manager, Reservations and Sales Agent; Tailor-Made Travel Specialists and DMCs: Tailor-Made Travel Specialist, DMC Ground Operations Manager; Travel Business Owners and Finance: Travel Company Owner, Finance and Administration Lead; Travelers and Trade Partners: Traveler and Booker, Reseller Travel Agent
Products: Group Travel ERP (B2B Vertical ERP SaaS): The GRIP-style back office for group and coach tour operators: product management for fixed-date departures and series, CRM, booking desk, invoicing and paymen…; Tailor-Made Travel Suite (B2B Vertical ERP SaaS): The TravelSpirit-style modular back office for tailor-made specialists and DMCs; Online Booking Websites and CMS (B2B2C Web): Real-time bookable customer websites under the operator's own brand (1TIS Klantwebsite, TravelSpirit SPoE Single Point of Entry) driven directly from the back-…; Traveler Portal and My-Trip App (B2B2C Portal and Mobile): The traveler's personal booking pages (booking inzien) and my-trip experience: approve quotes, pay open amounts, share passenger details, wishes and dietary re…; Supplier, DMC, Reseller and Guide Portals (B2B Partner Portals): The trade-facing portals that hang off the ERP: a supplier and DMC approval portal for one-to-one service requests, confirmations, room lists and passenger det…; Finance, Payments and Compliance (B2B SaaS Module): The finance and accounting attach: invoicing, payment links and alerts (Mollie), accounts receivable and payable, supplier settlement, accounting integration a…; Marketing and Business Intelligence (B2B SaaS Module): Marketing and steering on top of the integrated data model; AI Travel Office Assistant (AI Add-on): Embedded AI assistants for the travel office, the Vortex thesis of AI inside the ERP: drafting visual proposals from a travel wish and stored components, triag…
Product bricks: 30 building blocks in groups: Travel Product and Supply, Operations and Traveler Service, Sales and Booking, Finance and Insight
Data: Travel Product and Supply Data, Sales and Booking Data, Operations and Traveler Service Data, Finance and Compliance Data, Marketing and Platform Data
Delivery channels: Web Applications: Back-Office Web App, Visual Tour Builder Web, Customer Website and CMS, Traveler Portal Web, Supplier and DMC Portal Web, Reseller Portal Web, Guide Portal Web; Mobile Apps: Traveler App (Travelia, Appit), Guide Mobile; APIs and Integrations: Partner REST API, Accounting Connectors (Exact), Payment Connectors (Mollie), Flight and GDS Import (Airtrade), Channel and Bed-Bank Feeds (Airtrotter), Email Marketing Connector (Spotler, Trustpilot), Email and Telephony Integration (WeCloudIT), BI Export (Travel Intelligence); Internal Operations: Tenant Administration and Support Back Office
External systems: Transaction: Airtrade consolidator, IATA BSP, Mollie, Exact Online and accounting; Sales channels: Travelia and Appit traveler apps, e-Invoicing networks, Spotler, Mail platforms; Integration: Partner platforms; Data: Bed banks and Airtrotter, Exchange rate providers, Map tile and geocoding providers, Web analytics and tag managers; Service: Machine translation providers, Print and fulfilment partners, Hubby eSIM, Trustpilot
Competitors: 14 players, for example Lemax, Tourplan, Tourwriter

Composition:
One wide "context map" read from left to right:
- Left, outside the boundary: each customer group as ONE small cluster of two or three generic people
  with exactly one label, the group name. Do not split a group into individual roles or label single people.
- Center: one large rounded boundary for the domain. Inside it, the products as a row of distinct
  product cards on top; below them ONE shared row of building-block piles, one pile per product brick
  group, spanning the full width under all products. Products are assembled from many groups and groups
  are shared by many products, so do not pair a product with a group: no columns and no product-to-group
  arrows; the product row simply rests on the brick row. Below the bricks a shared data layer (a simple
  database shape) used by all brick groups.
- Along the bottom edge of the boundary: the delivery channels as a base strip of runtime platforms
  (servers, cloud, app stores) that the bricks are deployed to and the products are delivered through.
- Right, outside the boundary: the external systems as separate service tiles, each connected to the
  boundary with a two-way connector line.
- Bottom right, set apart and smaller: competitors as alternative storefronts the customers could choose instead,
  with a thin dashed line towards the customers, not connected to the domain.
- Arrows: customers <-> products; the brick row reads and writes the data layer;
  bricks are deployed onto the delivery channels;
  the domain exchanges data with the external systems. No other arrows.
