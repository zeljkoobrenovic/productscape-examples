Use case: scientific-educational
Asset type: one illustration in a plain-language product-domain tutorial for adults.
Domain: Transfer pricing: how companies explain prices inside a group
Reader: A curious adult arriving from another field. No background in tax, accounting, law, or business software is assumed.
Scope: An introduction to cross-border transfer pricing and the people, records, and software behind its annual documentation cycle. It uses the common framework of the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD), with an EU example in the history. Countries set their own legal requirements; OECD guidance is not a worldwide filing law. Detailed tax calculations, customs duties, and country-specific filing instructions are outside this guide. Luma, its people, countries A and B, and all amounts are fictional teaching examples.
Subject: Sofia prepares Luma's annual explanation
Setup: Follow one fictional lamp transaction within a fictional multinational group. Luma Manufacturing in country A supplies Luma Sales in country B. Sofia coordinates tax work; Leo supplies the seller's facts and accounts. The €60 cost, €80 internal price, €100 customer price, and €10 selling cost illustrate arithmetic only, not a benchmark or recommended price.
Step 1: Map the work and the obligations. Sofia identifies the companies, transaction, reporting year, and responsible people. With local specialists, she checks which reports and notifications apply, their deadlines, and whether they must be submitted or retained. Why: The team's task list comes from applicable requirements. Having a template does not make that document mandatory. Possible complication: None specified.
Step 2: Understand the business behind the contract. Leo explains ordering, storage, sales, and repairs. Legal colleagues provide the agreement. Sofia records who performs the work and makes decisions, checking the description with the people involved. Why: The price analysis needs an accurate business story before it needs polished wording. Possible complication: None specified.
Step 3: Develop and explain the analysis. Sofia and an adviser choose to examine seller profit relative to sales, recording why that method and the selected comparisons suit their assumed facts. Here €10 from €100 of sales is a 10% margin. Arithmetic alone does not establish whether that result is supportable. Why: A calculation can be correct even when the comparison or assumptions behind it are unsuitable. Possible complication: None specified.
Step 4: Check actual results and investigate a change. Leo connects the transaction figures to the accounts and explains differences. He also reports that the seller began authorizing and paying for warranty repairs during the year, while the earlier description assigned those responsibilities to the factory. Why: Sofia reopens the business description and analysis with the adviser instead of copying the old conclusion. Possible complication: The change may affect the analysis, but it does not by itself dictate a new price. The team checks the facts, timing, contracts, and relevant rules before deciding what to update.
Step 5: Prepare, localize, and review the documents. The team updates the repair description and affected analysis, then the platform assembles drafts. Sofia checks shared statements, Leo checks numbers, and local specialists check content and language. Reviewers resolve open issues before approving final versions. Why: A generated draft saves assembly work. It still needs checks that the explanation is accurate and locally suitable. Possible complication: None specified.
Step 6: Complete the obligations and keep the trail. The responsible people submit what is required through the relevant channels, keep confirmations, and retain other required records. Sofia archives the supporting work and records changes to revisit next year. Why: Completion includes evidence of submission where needed and the ability to answer later questions. An exported file alone proves neither. Possible complication: None specified.
Outcome: Luma has a reviewed explanation, traceable figures, and a record of completed obligations. The repair change is addressed rather than hidden in reused text. This case does not establish an acceptable real-world price or predict a tax authority's decision.

Teaching scene:
Six-panel adult teaching storyboard in a strict 3-column by 2-row grid, read left to right across top row then left to right across bottom row. Consistent characters: Sofia, woman with dark bob hair and teal jacket; Leo, man with short dark hair and amber sweater. Same simple desk lamp motif throughout. Panel 1 '1 Map': Sofia with two company cards and calendar. Panel 2 '2 Understand': Sofia interviewing Leo beside lamp stock and a contract. Panel 3 '3 Analyze': Sofia and adviser inspect comparison cards and calculator, no numbers. Panel 4 '4 Investigate change': Leo shows a broken lamp being repaired, Sofia revisits a folder, amber question mark, not disaster. Panel 5 '5 Review': both review a document at a laptop with another reviewer. Panel 6 '6 Submit and retain': report sent to a receiving tray plus separate archive folder, no authority approval. Exactly the six panel titles, no extra text, no percentages, no amounts, no flags, no progress arrows between panels, no courtroom or final victory stamp. Strong numbering and ample white space.

Style and composition:
- Aspect ratio 16:9, generous margins, bright warm-white background.
- A polished adult educational illustration: flat editorial drawing, crisp navy outlines,
  restrained teal, blue, and warm amber accents, subtle texture, very little shading.
- Make the concept understandable at the width of a reading column and on a phone.
- Use a small number of recognizable objects and people. Keep relationships spatially clear.
- Use an inclusive cast without occupational or cultural stereotypes.
- Short everyday labels are welcome where they teach the distinction. Use only labels
  explicitly requested in the teaching scene; otherwise keep the image free of text.
- Arrows must have a clear meaning and direction; do not connect unrelated comparison
  panels as though they are required stages. Do not imply certainty, causality, proportions,
  cash movements, or guaranteed outcomes that the explanation does not support.
- Keep all important content inside the frame. No headline, watermark, brand logo,
  decorative charts, invented statistics, jargon, tiny writing, photorealism, or 3D rendering.
- Explain the idea visually. The surrounding tutorial provides its full definition and caveats.
