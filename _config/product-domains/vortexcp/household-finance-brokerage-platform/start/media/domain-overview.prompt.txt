Draw the picture described below for the top of a product-domain website.
Domain: Household Finance Brokerage Platform
Reader: a product manager or engineer new to this domain who needs the big picture in ten seconds.

Background (for understanding only; do not write it in the image):
What the domain is: Covers the omnichannel comparison and brokerage business that Belgian households use to take control of their recurring costs: online simulators and comparison modules for mortgage loans, insurance (car, fire, funeral, outstanding-balance), energy and telecom; a network of about 70 franchised hypotheek.winkel offices where mortgage experts compare the conditions of 25 banks and guide buyers from feasibility certificate to notary deed; customer-care centres that compare, switch and cancel insurance, energy and telecom contracts by phone and chat; contract monitoring that warns households when a better deal exists; an academy that trains scarce mortgage and insurance advisors; and partner, tariff and commission operations with more than 50 banks, insurers and providers who pay the referral fees that keep the service free for consumers.
Customers: Households Comparing and Switching (3 customer roles); Advisory Network (3 customer roles); Partners and Providers (2 customer roles); Peasy Operations (1 customer roles)
Products: Peasy Comparison Platform (peasy.be) (Consumer Comparison Platform (Web, NL/FR)): peasy.be is the single consumer front door that Peasy placed in front of the hypotheek.winkel, Verzekeringen.be and Aanbieders.be brands in early 2025: mortgag…; hypotheek.winkel Mortgage Advice (Mortgage Brokerage Service (Office Network)): The mortgage brokerage that started in 2001 and today runs about 70 franchised hypotheek.winkel offices across Flanders and Brussels; Feasibility Certificate (Haalbaarheidscertificaat) (Consumer Document Service): The haalbaarheidscertificaat that a first-time buyer shows to sellers and estate agents to prove borrowing capacity before making an offer; Upload-Your-Proposal Review (Expert Review Service): A free second opinion on a mortgage or insurance proposal a household received from its own bank or another intermediary; Verzekeringen.be Insurance Comparison (Insurance Comparison and Brokerage (Web + Customer Care)): Verzekeringen.be / Assurances.be, acquired in 2021, compares car, fire, funeral, outstanding-balance and other household insurance across 11 insurers and lets…
Product bricks: 38 building blocks in groups: Consumer Comparison Platform, Mortgage Brokerage Workbench, Network and Partner Operations, Insurance, Energy and Telecom Brokerage
Data: Household, Identity and Consent Data, Consumer Comparison and Engagement Data, Mortgage Brokerage Data, Insurance, Energy and Telecom Brokerage Data, Network and Partner Operations Data, Compliance, Audit and Analytics Data
Delivery channels: Consumer Websites: peasy.be (Single Front Door), hypotheekwinkel.be (Mortgage Brokerage), verzekeringen.be / assurances.be (Insurance), aanbieders.be / mesfournisseurs.be (Energy and Telecom); Customer Account: Household Contract Vault, Savings Alerts and Notifications, Signing and Consent; Advisor Workbenches: Expert Workbench (hypotheek.winkel offices), Customer-Care Desk (Hasselt and Oujda), Knowledge Base and Scripts
External systems: Sales channels: E-mail and SMS provider, Video-call platform, Telephony and chat platform, E-mail and registered dispatch provider; Integration: Partner banks' credit systems, Insurer quote and bind APIs, Energy suppliers' switch interfaces, Telecom operators; Data: Google reviews and Trustpilot, Partner banks' credit systems and rate publications, FSMA registers of intermediaries, Insurer, energy supplier and telecom operator tariff feeds; Service: E-signature provider, Accounting and payment provider, Every platform brick
Competitors: 9 players, for example Immotheker Finotheker, Meilleurtaux Belgium, Credishop

Composition:
One wide "context map" read from left to right:
- Left, outside the boundary: each customer group as ONE small cluster of two or three generic people
  with exactly one label, the group name. Do not split a group into individual roles or label single people.
- Center: one large rounded boundary for the domain. Inside it, the products as a row of distinct
  product cards on top; below them ONE shared row of building-block piles, one pile per product brick
  group, spanning the full width under all products. Products are assembled from many groups and groups
  are shared by many products, so do not pair a product with a group: no columns and no product-to-group
  arrows; the product row simply rests on the brick row. Below the bricks a shared data layer (a simple
  database shape) used by all brick groups.
- Along the bottom edge of the boundary: the delivery channels as a base strip of runtime platforms
  (servers, cloud, app stores) that the bricks are deployed to and the products are delivered through.
- Right, outside the boundary: the external systems as separate service tiles, each connected to the
  boundary with a two-way connector line.
- Bottom right, set apart and smaller: competitors as alternative storefronts the customers could choose instead,
  with a thin dashed line towards the customers, not connected to the domain.
- Arrows: customers <-> products; the brick row reads and writes the data layer; bricks are deployed onto the delivery channels; the domain exchanges data with the external systems. No other arrows.

Style:
- Aspect ratio 16:9, generous margins, everything inside the frame.
- Background: pure white (#FFFFFF) across the whole canvas, with no cream, beige, paper tint,
  texture, or vignette. Only the domain boundary and the tiles may have a light fill.
- Clean flat editorial illustration with isometric hints: crisp navy outlines, restrained teal, blue,
  and warm amber accents, soft shadows, no photorealism and no 3D rendering.
- Outside elements and inside elements must be clearly distinguishable: the boundary separates them.
- Labels in a clear sans-serif, large enough to read at half size on a laptop screen.
- Generic, inclusive people and generic devices. No brand logos, trademarks, or watermarks anywhere,
  even for the named competitors, sales channels, and external systems: draw generic icons
  (a shopping cart, a storefront, a truck) instead of company marks. This includes app-store, browser,
  payment-card, and social-network marks, and product cards drawn as wordmarks.
