Use case: scientific-educational
Asset type: one illustration in a plain-language product-domain tutorial for adults.
Domain: Following the money: how a travel company's financial systems work
Reader: A curious adult from a different field who has never worked in accounting, payments or enterprise software. No knowledge of bookkeeping, SAP or software architecture is assumed.
Scope: An introduction to the financial systems behind a large online travel company: how a booking turns into payments, payouts, revenue, taxes and published financial statements, and why the software that does this needs deliberate design. Booking Holdings (Booking.com, Priceline, Agoda, KAYAK, OpenTable) is the running context, and facts about it come from its public filings and job postings. The guesthouse booking, the people and the amounts in the walkthrough are fictional. Traveler-facing apps, marketing and human-resources systems are outside this guide. Accounting and tax rules differ by country; the explanation describes general patterns for a United States-listed company that operates heavily in Europe.
Subject: Payment service provider, settlement and chargeback
Meaning: A payment service provider processes card and other online payments for a merchant. When a card is used online without being physically present (a card-not-present payment), the provider authorizes it and, days later, settles: it sends the money minus fees, usually as one batch with a file listing what is included. A chargeback is when the cardholder's bank reverses a payment because the cardholder disputes it.
Concrete example: A traveler pays 300 euros on Monday. On Thursday the provider deposits a single amount covering thousands of payments, after fees. Two months later the traveler's bank reverses the 300 euros because the traveler says the room was not as described.
Why this matters: The money does not arrive when, or in the amount, that the booking screen suggests. Alternative payment methods such as digital wallets and local bank transfers each have their own timing and file formats. Booking.com, for example, announced a partnership in 2025 with Ant International's Antom to add local payment methods in Asian markets. Each new method adds work to finance's ability to match money to bookings.

Teaching scene:
Three-part timeline left to right with headings 'Monday: payment', 'Thursday: settlement', 'Two months later: chargeback'. Part 1: a traveler pays 300 euros on a phone; an arrow goes to a box labeled 'Payment provider'. Part 2: one arrow from 'Payment provider' to a box labeled 'Company bank account', carrying a bundle labeled 'Batch: thousands of payments, minus fees', plus a document labeled 'Settlement file'. Part 3: one arrow from 'Company bank account' to a bank labeled 'Traveler's bank', labeled '300 reversed (dispute)'. Money in part 3 leaves the company; no arrow points back toward the company in part 3. Only these labels. Do not draw any real company logos or brand marks; use generic icons only. The traveler has no caption or name label.
