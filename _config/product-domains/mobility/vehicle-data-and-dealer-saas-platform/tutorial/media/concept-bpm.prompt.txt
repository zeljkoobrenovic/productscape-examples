Use case: scientific-educational
Asset type: one illustration in a plain-language product-domain tutorial for adults.
Domain: From license plate to sold car: how vehicle data powers the used-car trade
Reader: A curious adult from a different field who has bought or sold a car at most a few times. No knowledge of the car trade, Dutch regulation, or software is assumed.
Scope: An introduction to how professional used-car traders in the Netherlands use vehicle data and official registration services to buy, price, sell and transfer cars, and how private buyers use the same data to check a car. The Dutch rules (the RDW register, odometer registration, BPM tax) are local; other countries organise similar work differently. New-car sales, repairs, insurance claims handling and car financing itself are left out. VWE Automotive is one Dutch provider in this field; the explanation is meant to hold for the trade in general. The people and the car in the walkthrough are fictional, and its amounts are illustrative.
Subject: BPM
Meaning: A Dutch tax paid once when a passenger car is first registered in the Netherlands. For an imported used car, the tax is reduced for its age and depreciation, using a fixed table, a market price list or an individual valuation report.
Concrete example: A trader importing a three-year-old car from Germany must pay the remaining BPM before it gets a Dutch plate; choosing a well-founded valuation can lower the amount legitimately.
Why this matters: BPM can be thousands of euros, so a mistake can wipe out the profit on an imported car. Exporters can sometimes reclaim part of it.

Teaching scene:
Turn the concrete example into a simple visual explanation. Show the essential relationship or distinction, using recognizable people and objects. Choose one scene or two to three clearly separated panels only when comparison needs them.

Style and composition:
- Aspect ratio 16:9, generous margins, bright warm-white background.
- A polished adult educational illustration: flat editorial drawing, crisp navy outlines,
  restrained teal, blue, and warm amber accents, subtle texture, very little shading.
- Make the concept understandable at the width of a reading column and on a phone.
- Use a small number of recognizable objects and people. Keep relationships spatially clear.
- Use an inclusive cast without occupational or cultural stereotypes.
- Short everyday labels are welcome where they teach the distinction. Use only labels
  explicitly requested in the teaching scene; otherwise keep the image free of text.
- Arrows must have a clear meaning and direction; do not connect unrelated comparison
  panels as though they are required stages. Do not imply certainty, causality, proportions,
  cash movements, or guaranteed outcomes that the explanation does not support.
- Keep all important content inside the frame. No headline, watermark, brand logo,
  decorative charts, invented statistics, jargon, tiny writing, photorealism, or 3D rendering.
- Explain the idea visually. The surrounding tutorial provides its full definition and caveats.
