Draw the picture described below for the top of a product-domain website.
Domain: Digital Medication Management
Reader: a product manager or engineer new to this domain who needs the big picture in ten seconds.

Background (for understanding only; do not write it in the image):
What the domain is: The Digital Medication Management domain covers the patient, carer, care-provider, and pharmacy workflows required to run an automated medicine-dispensing and remote medication-management service for home care and supported living — spanning the in-home dispensing device, pharmacy multi-dose pouch logistics, remote adherence monitoring, missed-dose alarm escalation, care-team portals, and the regulated medical-device software platform behind them.
Customers: People Receiving Care: Home-Care Patient / Resident; Front-Line Care Staff: Home-Care Nurse / Carer; Care Providers and Funders: Care-Provider / Municipality Manager; Supply Partners: Pharmacy Dose-Packaging Partner
Products: Evondos Anna Dispensing Service (B2G / B2B Managed Medical-Device Service); Evondos Telecare Portal (B2B SaaS (Remote Care)); Medido Home Dispensing (B2G / B2B Managed Dispensing Service); Pharmacy Supply Connect (Supply Partner Platform); Care Outcome Insights (B2G Analytics and Evidence)
Product bricks: 24 building blocks in groups: Deployment, Fleet and Platform, In-Home Dispensing, Remote Care and Monitoring, Pharmacy and Medication Supply
Data: Patient and Care Data, Dispensing and Adherence Data, Device and Fleet Data, Care Organization Data, Pharmacy Supply Data, Governance, Identity, and Analytics Data
Delivery channels: EU Public Cloud: Dispensing and Device Cloud, Remote Care Cloud, Pharmacy Supply Cloud, Deployment and Fleet Cloud, Shared Platform and Governance Cloud; In-Home Dispensing Devices: Dispensing Device Fleet; Mobile App Stores: iOS App Store, Android App Store
External systems: Connectivity: Mobile network operators; Supply: Pharmacy multi-dose packaging machines; Identity providers: Care-organization identity providers; Cloud infrastructure: EU cloud infrastructure provider; Sales channels: SMS and voice gateways; Data: National e-prescription services, Electronic care record systems; Partner: Municipal case-management systems; Platform Consumer: All internal experiences
Competitors: 8 players, for example iZafe Group, Spencer Health Solutions, Hero Health

Composition:
One wide "context map" read from left to right:
- Left, outside the boundary: each customer group as ONE small cluster of two or three generic people
  with exactly one label, the group name. Do not split a group into individual roles or label single people.
- Center: one large rounded boundary for the domain. Inside it, the products as a row of distinct
  product cards on top; below them ONE shared row of building-block piles, one pile per product brick
  group, spanning the full width under all products. Products are assembled from many groups and groups
  are shared by many products, so do not pair a product with a group: no columns and no product-to-group
  arrows; the product row simply rests on the brick row. Below the bricks a shared data layer (a simple
  database shape) used by all brick groups.
- Along the bottom edge of the boundary: the delivery channels as a base strip of runtime platforms
  (servers, cloud, app stores) that the bricks are deployed to and the products are delivered through.
- Right, outside the boundary: the external systems as separate service tiles, each connected to the
  boundary with a two-way connector line.
- Bottom right, set apart and smaller: competitors as alternative storefronts the customers could choose instead,
  with a thin dashed line towards the customers, not connected to the domain.
- Arrows: customers <-> products; the brick row reads and writes the data layer;
  bricks are deployed onto the delivery channels;
  the domain exchanges data with the external systems. No other arrows.
