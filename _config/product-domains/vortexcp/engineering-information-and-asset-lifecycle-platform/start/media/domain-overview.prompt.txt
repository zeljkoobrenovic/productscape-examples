Draw the picture described below for the top of a product-domain website.
Domain: Engineering Information and Asset Lifecycle Platform
Reader: a product manager or engineer new to this domain who needs the big picture in ten seconds.

Background (for understanding only; do not write it in the image):
What the domain is: Assai-inspired engineering information platform connecting controlled documents, project handover, asset context and verified engineering change across capital projects and operations. Public product evidence is separated from proposed strategy, architecture and operating-model assumptions.
Customers: Capital project accountability: Asset Owner Programme Director, EPC Engineering Manager; Engineering information delivery: Lead Document Controller, Supplier Documentation Coordinator; Asset operations and assurance: Maintenance and Reliability Engineer, Asset Information Assurance Manager; Digital enablement and service adoption: Enterprise Application and Security Owner, Implementation and Migration Lead
Products: Assai DMS (B2B engineering document control): Published software offer: controlled engineering records, reviews, transmittals and lifecycle continuity; Assai Viewport (B2B asset information access): Published software offer: asset context across existing systems; a standalone deployment can use external repositories without purchasing Assai DMS; Assai Enterprise (B2B engineering change governance): Published software offer: coordination and verification of information updates across affected systems; Implementation, Migration and Enablement Services (Professional services supporting software adoption): Modeled service offer based on published implementation support; this is not an asserted separately licensed software SKU
Product bricks: 24 building blocks in groups: Project information control, Service trust and adoption, Asset context and interoperability
Data: Project information control, Asset context and interoperability, Service trust and adoption
Delivery channels: Managed Software Runtime: Controlled Engineering Records Runtime, Asset Context and Change Runtime, Integration Runtime, Shared Service and Adoption Runtime; Browser Distribution: Project and Supplier Workspaces, Asset Context and Change Workspaces, Administration and Implementation Workspace
External systems: Integration: Email or customer-agreed delivery gateway, Customer DMS, EAM/CMMS, historian and BI endpoints, Customer identity provider, Cloud hosting and monitoring services; provider not established
Competitors: 8 players, for example Oracle Aconex, Bentley ProjectWise, AVEVA Asset Information

Composition:
One wide "context map" read from left to right:
- Left, outside the boundary: each customer group as ONE small cluster of two or three generic people
  with exactly one label, the group name. Do not split a group into individual roles or label single people.
- Center: one large rounded boundary for the domain. Inside it, the products as a row of distinct
  product cards on top; below them ONE shared row of building-block piles, one pile per product brick
  group, spanning the full width under all products. Products are assembled from many groups and groups
  are shared by many products, so do not pair a product with a group: no columns and no product-to-group
  arrows; the product row simply rests on the brick row. Below the bricks a shared data layer (a simple
  database shape) used by all brick groups.
- Along the bottom edge of the boundary: the delivery channels as a base strip of runtime platforms
  (servers, cloud, app stores) that the bricks are deployed to and the products are delivered through.
- Right, outside the boundary: the external systems as separate service tiles, each connected to the
  boundary with a two-way connector line.
- Bottom right, set apart and smaller: competitors as alternative storefronts the customers could choose instead,
  with a thin dashed line towards the customers, not connected to the domain.
- Arrows: customers <-> products; the brick row reads and writes the data layer; bricks are deployed onto the delivery channels; the domain exchanges data with the external systems. No other arrows.
