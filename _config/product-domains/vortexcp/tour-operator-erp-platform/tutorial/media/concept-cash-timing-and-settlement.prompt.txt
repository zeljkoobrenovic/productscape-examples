Use case: scientific-educational
Asset type: one illustration in a plain-language product-domain tutorial for adults.
Domain: From a travel idea to a trip that works
Reader: Someone arriving from another field, with no assumed knowledge of travel businesses, accounting, or business software.
Scope: An introduction to the work of tour operators and the software that connects planning, sales, suppliers, travellers, and finance. Examples reflect group and tailor-made travel in a European setting. Legal duties depend on the arrangement, country, and applicable dates. This is not a guide to running an airline or choosing a holiday. Nora, Coast & Village Travel, the trip, and all prices and contract terms are fictional.
Subject: Cash timing and settlement
Meaning: Cash timing concerns when money actually arrives and leaves. A deposit is an advance payment, not necessarily the whole price. Settlement means checking and completing amounts owed. Reconciliation means matching records, such as a bank receipt to the booking it pays for.
Concrete example: The six travellers pay €45 deposits, bringing in €270. Suppose supplier advances of €350 are due before the remaining customer payments. The operator must plan the €80 timing gap, even though the trip is expected to have a positive margin.
Why this matters: Expected margin does not pay a bill today. Finance must also follow applicable rules for protecting customer money and handle refunds or disputed amounts correctly.

Teaching scene:
A simple two-stage timeline about payment timing. First stage heading 'Now' has two separate cards labeled 'Customer deposits €270' and 'Supplier advances €350'. Beneath them an amber bracket labeled 'Timing gap €80'. Second stage heading 'Later' has a customer payment envelope labeled 'Remaining customer payments'. One neutral time arrow from Now to Later. Do not depict customer cash being automatically forwarded, do not add balances, dates, percentages or lending advice, and do not label the gap a loss. Exactly these six labels. Clean adult diagram.

Style and composition:
- Aspect ratio 16:9, generous margins, bright warm-white background.
- A polished adult educational illustration: flat editorial drawing, crisp navy outlines,
  restrained teal, blue, and warm amber accents, subtle texture, very little shading.
- Make the concept understandable at the width of a reading column and on a phone.
- Use a small number of recognizable objects and people. Keep relationships spatially clear.
- Use an inclusive cast without occupational or cultural stereotypes.
- Short everyday labels are welcome where they teach the distinction. Use only labels
  explicitly requested in the teaching scene; otherwise keep the image free of text.
- Arrows must have a clear meaning and direction; do not connect unrelated comparison
  panels as though they are required stages. Do not imply certainty, causality, proportions,
  cash movements, or guaranteed outcomes that the explanation does not support.
- Keep all important content inside the frame. No headline, watermark, brand logo,
  decorative charts, invented statistics, jargon, tiny writing, photorealism, or 3D rendering.
- Explain the idea visually. The surrounding tutorial provides its full definition and caveats.
