Use case: scientific-educational
Asset type: one illustration in a plain-language product-domain tutorial for adults.
Domain: Selling under your own name: how a merchant commerce platform works
Reader: A curious adult coming from a different field who has never run a shop online. No knowledge of retail, payments, or software is assumed.
Scope: An introduction to platforms that let independent merchants run their own branded shop online and in person, using Shopify as the best-known example. It follows one order from a shopper's first visit to the merchant being paid, and covers the platform's role in checkout, payments, stock, shipping, and its ecosystem of add-on apps. Marketplaces where the platform sells to the shopper itself (such as Amazon's retail business), warehouse operations, and detailed tax or customs law are outside this guide. Payment rules, taxes, and import duties differ by country; examples that depend on them are marked as local. The shop, people, and numbers in the walkthrough are fictional.
Subject: Merchant of record
Meaning: The business that is legally selling the goods and is responsible for refunds, taxes, and the promise made to the shopper.
Concrete example: When a mug arrives broken, the shopper's claim is against the mug maker, not against the platform that ran the checkout.
Why this matters: It explains who answers for what. The platform provides tools and processes the payment, but the merchant carries the commercial and legal responsibility for the sale.

Teaching scene:
Show a shopper holding a parcel with a cracked bowl, an arrow labeled 'refund request' going to a small ceramics studio labeled 'Merchant (seller)'. In the background, a separate building labeled 'Platform' with a dashed line to the studio labeled 'tools and payments'. The platform must clearly not be the one receiving the complaint.

Style and composition:
- Aspect ratio 16:9, generous margins, bright warm-white background.
- A polished adult educational illustration: flat editorial drawing, crisp navy outlines,
  restrained teal, blue, and warm amber accents, subtle texture, very little shading.
- Make the concept understandable at the width of a reading column and on a phone.
- Use a small number of recognizable objects and people. Keep relationships spatially clear.
- Use an inclusive cast without occupational or cultural stereotypes.
- Short everyday labels are welcome where they teach the distinction. Use only labels
  explicitly requested in the teaching scene; otherwise keep the image free of text.
- Arrows must have a clear meaning and direction; do not connect unrelated comparison
  panels as though they are required stages. Do not imply certainty, causality, proportions,
  cash movements, or guaranteed outcomes that the explanation does not support.
- Keep all important content inside the frame. No headline, watermark, brand logo,
  decorative charts, invented statistics, jargon, tiny writing, photorealism, or 3D rendering.
- Explain the idea visually. The surrounding tutorial provides its full definition and caveats.
