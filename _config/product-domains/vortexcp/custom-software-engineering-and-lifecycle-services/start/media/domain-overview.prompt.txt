Draw the picture described below for the top of a product-domain website.
Domain: Custom Software Engineering & Lifecycle Services
Reader: a product manager or engineer new to this domain who needs the big picture in ten seconds.

Background (for understanding only; do not write it in the image):
What the domain is: A Dawn Technology-inspired landscape for validating, building, modernizing and sustaining client software, connecting customer outcomes to accountable engineering, assurance and lifecycle delivery.
Customers: Investment and Product Leadership: Client CIO and Investment Sponsor, Digital Product and Experience Owner; Engineering and Service Owners: Client Architect and Engineering Lead, Client Service and Operations Owner; Assurance and Data Owners: Medical Software Product and Quality Lead, Data and AI Product Owner; Service Beneficiaries: Digital Service User and Frontline Worker; Delivery Ecosystem: Dawn Engagement and Chapter Lead
Products: IT Advisory (Professional service): Architecture and investment advice for a client’s software estate; roadmap and ownership controls are modeled deliverables; Ideation & Discovery (Discovery engagement): Research, problem framing, prototypes and validation before committing to a full software build; Custom Software Development (Client-specific software delivery): Bespoke web, mobile, smart-device and business applications, with integration and modernization where required; Infrastructure Design, Build & Hosting (Infrastructure and hosting service): Design and operation of the selected public, private or client-controlled environment; the deployment patterns here are proposed; Quality Control (Software quality service): Testing and evaluation of application behavior against agreed requirements and operational expectations
Product bricks: 22 building blocks in groups: Engineering Quality and Lifecycle, Client Application Engineering, Discovery and Delivery Control, Data and Grounded Assistance
Data: Client Delivery and Service Records, Approved Shared Engineering Records
Delivery channels: Professional Delivery: Client Delivery and Assurance Workspace; Client Application Runtime: Client-controlled Application and Data Environment, Web, Mobile and Smart-device Experiences; Contracted Operations: Lifecycle Service Workspace
External systems: External System: Client research and design tools, Client business systems, Client contract and procurement records, People and professional-services systems
Competitors: 5 players, for example Info Support, Furore Conclusion, Xebia

Composition:
One wide "context map" read from left to right:
- Left, outside the boundary: each customer group as ONE small cluster of two or three generic people
  with exactly one label, the group name. Do not split a group into individual roles or label single people.
- Center: one large rounded boundary for the domain. Inside it, the products as a row of distinct
  product cards on top; below them ONE shared row of building-block piles, one pile per product brick
  group, spanning the full width under all products. Products are assembled from many groups and groups
  are shared by many products, so do not pair a product with a group: no columns and no product-to-group
  arrows; the product row simply rests on the brick row. Below the bricks a shared data layer (a simple
  database shape) used by all brick groups.
- Along the bottom edge of the boundary: the delivery channels as a base strip of runtime platforms
  (servers, cloud, app stores) that the bricks are deployed to and the products are delivered through.
- Right, outside the boundary: the external systems as separate service tiles, each connected to the
  boundary with a two-way connector line.
- Bottom right, set apart and smaller: competitors as alternative storefronts the customers could choose instead,
  with a thin dashed line towards the customers, not connected to the domain.
- Arrows: customers <-> products; the brick row reads and writes the data layer; bricks are deployed onto the delivery channels; the domain exchanges data with the external systems. No other arrows.
